7 Cheap Espresso Machines That Actually Make Great Coffee

Cheap espresso machines have a bad reputation, and most of it is earned. But a few budget machines pull genuinely good shots, and we found them. Every machine below costs under 300 dollars, makes real espresso with crema, and survived a week of daily use in our kitchen.

1. Breville Bambino

The Bambino is the smallest machine here and the fastest to heat up: ready in three seconds. It has a 15 bar pump, automatic milk frothing, and a compact footprint that fits any counter. The shots are sweet and balanced, and the steam wand is the easiest to learn in this list.

2. De'Longhi Dedica

The Dedica is slim, quiet and good looking, and it makes a solid double shot with the pressurised basket. The manual steam wand takes practice, but the coffee quality for the price is hard to beat. It is the best pick if your kitchen is short on space.

3. Gaggia Classic Pro

The Classic Pro is the enthusiast favourite: a real 58mm portafilter, a commercial style steam wand, and a machine you can repair and upgrade for years. It needs a good grinder to shine, so budget for one if you do not own one yet.

4. Breville Barista Express Impress

The Impress adds a built-in grinder and assisted tamping to the Bambino formula, which removes the two hardest skills for a beginner. It costs more than the others here, but it replaces a separate grinder that would cost the same on its own.

5. Nespresso Vertuo Plus

The Vertuo is not real espresso in the strict sense, but it makes a consistent, tasty cup with zero skill and zero mess. If you value convenience over craft, this is the honest pick: pods cost more per cup, but the machine is cheap and the coffee is always good.

6. De'Longhi Stilosa

The Stilosa is the cheapest machine on this list and the most surprising. With fresh beans and a decent grind it pulls a respectable shot, and the manual frother makes acceptable milk. It is the best way to find out whether home espresso is for you without spending much.

7. Gevi Espresso Machine

The Gevi is a dark horse: 20 bar pump, a built-in milk frother, and a price that undercuts everything else here. The build feels light and the instructions are poor, but the coffee in the cup is better than the price suggests.

How to choose a cheap espresso machine

Choose by your grinder situation first. If you own a burr grinder, buy the best machine-only option you can afford. If you do not, buy a machine with a built-in grinder or a pressurised basket, which forgives a coarse grind. Then check the counter space, the water tank size, and whether the drip tray is easy to clean, because you will do it every day.

What is the best budget espresso machine?

For most people, the Breville Bambino is the best budget espresso machine: fast, small, easy to use, and it makes great coffee with milk. If you want the lowest price, the De'Longhi Stilosa is the best cheap option. If you want a machine to grow with, the Gaggia Classic Pro is the one to buy.

How much should you spend on your first espresso machine?

Spend 150 to 300 dollars on the machine and keep 50 to 100 dollars aside for fresh beans and a tamper. The beans matter more than the last 50 dollars of machine, so do not spend your whole budget on the box.
